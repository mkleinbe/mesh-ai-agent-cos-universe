# CFO Operating Cadence and Executive Artifacts

Use this reference for recurring management-finance operating rhythm and reusable finance outputs. It does not create close, GL, treasury, audit, tax, payment, or bank-balance authority.

## Weekly exception cadence

Focus on decisions and exceptions, not ritual reporting. Where approved evidence exists, review:
- cash/runway or short-horizon cash exposure;
- AR aging, DSO, invoice and collections timing;
- pipeline-to-revenue timing assumptions supplied by the authoritative commercial owner;
- engagement margin, utilization, cost-to-serve, and delivery-economics exceptions;
- forecast driver movement and material variance triggers;
- upcoming spending, hiring, pricing, or investment decisions that require business cases or human approval.

Do not invent thresholds. Use approved targets, contractual terms, prior approved plan values, or clearly labeled analytical ranges.

## Monthly management FP&A cadence

Produce a driver-based forecast-versus-actual view that separates volume, price/rate, mix, cost, timing, scope, and assumption error when the source supports them. Include:
- current outlook versus plan and prior forecast;
- engagement, service-line, customer, or firm-management contribution economics at the authorized grain;
- unit economics where definitions are supportable;
- working-capital and cash-timing implications;
- material concentration or dependency exposure;
- investment/hiring/tool/partner business cases requiring a decision;
- top assumptions that can reverse the recommendation.

Management FP&A does not turn management artifacts into enterprise ledger truth.

## Quarterly decision cadence

Refresh only decision-relevant longer-horizon views:
- base and downside scenarios;
- capital-allocation alternatives;
- service-line or portfolio economics;
- concentration and resilience exposure;
- forecast range and material break points;
- value realization from prior approved investments;
- unresolved evidence gaps or policy decisions requiring CEO/human authority.

## Executive artifacts

Route quantitative production and validation through `mesh-data-analytics` when the output is material or durable.

### CFO scorecard
Include the smallest set of metrics required for the decision, with source, period, definition, actual/plan/forecast state, variance, confidence, and concise implication.

### Rolling forecast
Show operational drivers, plan/prior/current forecast, scenario ranges, material assumptions, and reconciliation to the approved management source.

### 13-week cash view
Use only supported management-finance cash-in/cash-out inputs. Separate committed, probable, and assumed flows. Label it an analytical forecast, never a treasury or bank record.

### Investment business case
Include alternatives, cost of inaction, cash flows, fit-for-purpose ROI/NPV/IRR/payback/break-even, sensitivity, qualitative benefits separated from quantified benefits, reversal cost, approval status, and next owner.

### Model review / valuation packet
Include purpose, source set, mechanical QA, normalization, method selection, assumptions, tie-outs, sensitivity, range, confidence, unresolved data gaps, and explicit non-audit status.

### CEO or board finance brief
Lead with the decision or material consequence. Show the few drivers that explain the position, downside exposure, decisions required, evidence quality, and approval owner. Do not bury unresolved source or model limitations.

## Artifact controls
- Bind every chart and table to the same validated analytical dataset as the written conclusion.
- Preserve units, periods, denominators, source, as-of date, actual/plan/forecast/scenario state, and confidence.
- Do not manually re-enter material figures when a reproducible analytical path is available.
- Never claim a durable artifact is validated because it merely rendered or opened.
- External/client/public release remains human-controlled and follows Mesh design and artifact governance when applicable.
