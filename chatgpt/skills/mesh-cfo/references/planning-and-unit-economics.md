# Planning, Cash, and Unit Economics

Apply this reference only to supported Engagement Finance / FP&A work. Management-finance analysis does not create enterprise cash, treasury, accounting, or balance-sheet authority.

## Driver-based planning

Build forecasts from operational drivers instead of applying unexplained percentage growth. Select drivers that causally connect to the supported economics, such as:
- contracted or weighted revenue and timing;
- billable capacity, utilization, rate, and delivery mix;
- headcount, contractor cost, and time to productivity;
- customer acquisition, expansion, retention, or churn when supported;
- collections timing and payment terms;
- recurring versus non-recurring cost drivers.

For each driver record source, owner, period, confidence, and whether it is actual, committed, forecast, or assumption.

## Forecast versus actual

Compare actual outcomes with the forecast at the driver and result levels. Separate:
- volume variance;
- price/rate variance;
- mix variance;
- cost variance;
- timing variance;
- scope variance;
- assumption error.

Do not hide offsetting variances inside a net total. Surface the few drivers that explain the material economic movement.

## Unit economics

Use contribution economics rather than revenue-only economics.

Common measures include:
- contribution per engagement, customer, unit, or delivery hour;
- gross or contribution margin;
- CAC when a supported acquisition-cost denominator exists;
- LTV when retention/churn history and contribution economics support a defensible estimate;
- LTV:CAC as a diagnostic ratio, not an approval rule;
- CAC payback based on contribution margin rather than revenue alone.

Segment the analysis when blended averages hide materially different channels, offerings, cohorts, or engagement types.

### LTV and CAC caution

LTV is highly assumption-sensitive. State the retention or churn basis, margin basis, forecast horizon, and discounting convention. Do not present a long extrapolated lifetime as observed fact.

CAC must be explicit about included sales and marketing labor, tools, partner fees, media, and other acquisition costs. Do not compare differently defined CAC measures without reconciliation.

## Cash runway and burn

When the user provides or authorizes supported management-finance cash inputs, the CFO may model:

`Runway = Supported Available Cash / Net Cash Burn per Period`

where a positive net burn means cash consumption. If the company is cash generative, do not force a runway construct that obscures the actual condition.

Burn analysis should identify:
- gross cash outflow;
- supported recurring inflow;
- net burn or generation;
- one-time items;
- timing concentration;
- scenario thresholds.

Never claim a bank balance, unrestricted enterprise cash balance, or treasury position from an analytical model.

## Working capital

Where the evidence supports the underlying accounts or engagement-level timing, use:

`Cash Conversion Cycle = DIO + DSO - DPO`

For professional-services economics, DIO may be irrelevant. Prefer the actual supported drivers, often DSO, unbilled work, milestone timing, contractor/payment timing, and DPO.

### DSO

Use DSO to assess collections timing only when revenue and receivables definitions are compatible. For engagement analysis, invoice aging and contractual payment terms can be more decision-useful than an enterprise aggregate.

### DPO

Use DPO only when supported payable data exists. Do not recommend delaying contractual obligations or changing payment practice without the appropriate human owner.

## 13-week and rolling cash views

A 13-week cash forecast can be useful for short-horizon management planning when supported cash-in and cash-out data is provided. It is an analytical forecast, not a bank or treasury record. Longer-range rolling forecasts should be driver-based and clearly separate committed, probable, and assumed cash flows.

## Benchmark discipline

A benchmark is reference evidence, not a target by default. For any benchmark:
1. cite the source and date;
2. confirm comparable company type, scale, geography, business model, and metric definition;
3. state whether the benchmark is descriptive, aspirational, or policy-approved;
4. show the Mesh-specific actual and decision implication separately.

Donor thresholds such as LTV:CAC, payback, Rule of 40, revenue per employee, runway, department spending percentages, churn, customer concentration, DSO, or reserve levels are not Mesh policy unless independently adopted by the qualified human authority.

## Capital-allocation and hiring questions

For a proposed hire, tool, partner, or operating investment, analyze:
- incremental cost and timing;
- direct revenue or contribution effect where evidence exists;
- productivity leverage;
- time to productivity or benefit realization;
- payback and NPV when appropriate;
- alternative uses of the same capital;
- downside case and reversal cost.

Return a recommendation and decision conditions. Do not authorize hiring, spending, pricing, or other consequential commitments.
